The problem

Most recovery systems
just keep trying.

Card declined? Retry tomorrow. Retry the day after. Then email a payment-update link. Every attempt is treated as free — no cost for the network fee it triggers, the issuer trust it burns, or the customer it eventually pushes away.

The thesis

Every attempt has a price.
Backstop pays it correctly.

Before it retries, contacts, or escalates, Backstop prices the action in rupees — expected recovery, minus network penalties, minus issuer risk, minus the chance this exact contact is the one that costs the customer. Stop isn't a special rule. It's just the action that scores zero.

Each layer may only narrow what the previous layer proposed — veto it, delay it, soften it. None of them can make the system do more than what already came through. That's the actual safety mechanism, enforced in code, not just claimed in a diagram.
